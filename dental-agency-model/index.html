Meta lead gen for European dental clinics

Dental Ads Unit Economics

One question: what commission can we charge a dental clinic that is fair for them and worth it for us. The clinic pays Meta directly. We run the ads, the funnel and the sales process up to the chair, they treat, we take a share of the revenue we can prove. Every number on this page comes from the sliders. The presets are planning assumptions, not measured results. The Clinica Duchev preset uses what their site publishes.

Scenario

One clinic, one month

Leads come from a mix of Meta instant forms and a landing page funnel firing a custom conversion. Our setter calls them within minutes, qualifies them and books the consult. The clinic only sees people who turn up. Each step is a slider.

Where each euro of treatment revenue goes

The honest picture for the clinic owner. Revenue is accepted treatments plus what every show pays at the first visit. Treatment cost is lab, materials and clinician time. What is left after ads and our fee is their profit on the patients we brought.

Under 20% of revenue: clinic wins big20 to 33%: fair for bothOver 33%: tight, over 50%: no deal

Our rule for the pitch: all in, ads plus us, the clinic keeps at least 3 euros of treatment revenue for every euro out. That is a 3x blended ROAS, or marketing at a third of revenue. Under 3x they feel it every month. Under 2x they leave. Over 5x we are underpriced.

The first clinic: Clinica Duchev, Sofia

What their own site and the press say, and what it changes in the model. The Clinica Duchev preset in the scenario switcher loads the numbers below. Prices are in euros at the fixed 1.95583 lev rate.

Who they are. Dental Clinic Petar Duchev, Mladost IV in Sofia, a 1,100 square metre building opened in 2021 with separate adult, paediatric and hygiene units and an operating theatre for sedation. Founder Dr Petar Duchev, Nobel Biocare lecturer, X-Guide navigated implant surgery, All-on-4 same-day arches, veneers by a visiting Italian ceramist, braces, general anaesthesia for adults and children. They say it out loud: premium segment, premium mark-up. Google sits at 4.6 stars on about 116 reviews. Reported 2021 turnover around 1.25 million euros.

What a first visit costs. This is the part that matters for the model. An adult cannot get treated without a 3D scan the day before (400 lev, 204.52 euros) and a 60 to 90 minute consultation with Dr Duchev himself (300 lev, 153.39 euros). That is 357.91 euros before anyone talks about an implant. Add the recommended hygiene visit at 245.42 euros and the diagnostic pathway at 255.65 and a total rehab candidate has paid around 860 euros before treatment starts. Only "just asking" at reception is free, and it usually needs a current X-ray anyway. So the 200 euro floor is right in spirit and low in number. The preset uses 358.

What the paid consult does to the funnel. A cold Meta lead who learns the first visit costs 358 euros books less often than one offered a free consult. Because our setter runs the whole conversation, frames the 358 as the diagnostic it is and books the scan, the preset uses 30% lead to booked rather than the 20% a front desk would get. People who have paid for a scan the day before show up, so show rate is 80%, and they are pre-qualified on money, so case acceptance sits at 35%. Fewer, better patients. The instant form is the top of the funnel and the setter call is the middle. Leads at 15 euros in Sofia are realistic for a Bulgarian-language lead form. At 500 euros of spend that is about 33 leads and 8 people in Dr Duchev's chair a month.

Where the money is. The tiles above split our fee into the front end and the back end. At the current sliders the 358 euro diagnostics alone would pay us a few hundred a month. The accepted cases pay the rest. That is why the contract has to give us the treatment value, and why the next section is the one to get right before anything else.

Capacity is the real ceiling. Every adult consultation is done by Dr Duchev personally, 60 to 90 minutes each, Monday to Friday nine to five. That is somewhere between 10 and 20 new consults a week at the very most, before his surgery days. The tile above shows how many consults a week the current sliders send him. Past 8 to 10 a week we should stop scaling spend and start talking about a second consulting dentist, otherwise booking lead times stretch and show rate falls.

They are not running ads today. The Meta Ad Library shows three ads in the page's history, all for the children's clinic, the last two in December 2025 offering free children's consultations. Zero adult, implant or veneer ads ever. The site was last updated in 2020, has a Meta pixel, Google tag manager and a Google Ads conversion tag, and takes bookings by phone or a contact form. No online booking, no WhatsApp, no landing pages, no financing, no offers for adults. The Bulgarian and English pages even disagree on the hygiene price (330 versus 480 lev). All of that is work we do in the setup phase and it is why the setup fee is not optional here.

What they pay today, and how ours sits next to it. The clinic pays the content partner 2,400 euros a month: 1,900 for a photographer coming in to shoot with Dr Duchev, editing and organic scripting, and 500 earmarked for ads. Nothing is being spent on ads right now. Our deal does not replace the content work, it uses it. That footage is the ad creative. What we change is that the 500 actually gets spent, the leads get called, and the clinic pays us nothing unless a patient pays them. At the current sliders that is a month for around of revenue they do not have today, against a flat 2,400 that produces content and no patients. Once the pilot proves the numbers, the case for moving spend from 500 to 1,500 makes itself, and the ladder above shows what that does to our fee.

What to change in the contract for them. Total rehab cases run over months and complex ones over one to two years, so a 180 day attribution window undersells us. Ask for twelve months on the lead date, commission on payments as they are collected. Reconciliation is easy because one man does every adult consult: the lead sheet against his diary once a month. And since the diagnostics are paid, agree that the 358 euros count as revenue for the commission. It is real money the clinic did not have.

Getting paid on the back end

Commission only works if we can see the treatment value, not just the 358 at the door. Because we run the sales process we are in a better position than most agencies: we know every patient by name before the clinic does. Here is how to lock it in, in the order that matters.

  • We own the front door. Every lead is booked by our setter through our booking page, and the 358 euro diagnostic is paid there, by card, before the visit. That does three things: proves the front end automatically, gives us the patient's name, phone and email as the record of origin, and filters out people who were never going to buy. The clinic gets the money passed through weekly, or we settle it against the commission invoice.
  • Origin is defined in the contract, not argued monthly. Anyone who first booked through our system, or whose phone or email matches a lead of ours within 30 days, is our patient for twelve months from the lead date, across every treatment they accept. No "they saw a friend's Instagram" carve-outs. It is a one-paragraph clause and it is the whole deal.
  • Our setter stays in the process after the consult. The day after the plan presentation, our setter calls the patient: did you get the plan, what did it come to, any questions, do you want the financing option explained. That call lifts acceptance and it tells us the plan value from the patient's own mouth. It is the cross-check on whatever the clinic reports.
  • One shared list, updated monthly by the clinic. A sheet, or a view in our CRM, with our patients down the side and four columns for the clinic: plan presented (value), accepted (value), collected this month, remaining. Dr Duchev does every adult consult himself, so the diary and the practice software are one source. Ten minutes a month for his office manager. If the numbers stop coming, the contract says commission falls back to a fixed fee per patient who showed, at a rate set from this model.
  • Commission on collected, with a twelve month tail. Big cases pay in stages over months. We invoice our share of whatever was collected from our patients that month, and keep invoicing for twelve months after the lead date. Financed cases count when the finance company pays out. No commission on quotes, so the clinic never pays on money it has not seen, and no reason for them to under-report a plan.
  • Read access, once trust exists. After the pilot, ask for a read-only login or a monthly export from the practice management software filtered to our patient list. Most practice systems can do a patient-level payments export. It turns reconciliation from a conversation into a download.
  • If they will not share the back end at all. Then commission is a handshake and we should not price it as one. The fallback is a tiered case fee paid on acceptance, priced from this model: one figure for a single implant case, a bigger one for a multi-implant or veneer case, a bigger one again for All-on-4. It approximates the percentage without needing the invoice. Less fair than a true percentage in both directions, which is why it is the fallback.

The short version for the sales call: "We book them, we take the diagnostic payment, we follow up after the plan. You tell us once a month what our patients paid you. We take of that for twelve months. If you stop telling us, we switch to a flat fee per patient in the chair." Said like that it sounds like what it is, a partner who only gets paid when they do.

What to charge, and how to say it

Deal terms to put in the contract

  • The clinic pays Meta directly from its own ad account. We get admin access. No media money touches our books, no cash flow risk, no VAT mess on pass-through spend.
  • Minimum ad spend of 2,000 euros a month for the pilot. Below that the algorithm never gets enough conversions to learn and both sides waste 90 days.
  • Setup fee covers funnel, creative and CRM build. Charge one even though it is not in the model. It filters out tire kickers and pays for the hours before the first lead lands.
  • Commission is on collected revenue from patients we sourced, not on quotes. Financed cases count when the financing pays out. Matched by phone or email against the lead sheet, reconciled monthly from their practice software.
  • Attribution window of 180 days from lead date. Implant decisions take time. Six months is fair to both sides.
  • Consult slots clause. We do the calling and booking. The clinic commits to a minimum number of new-patient consult slots a week and to presenting the plan within seven days of the scan. If slots dry up, booked patients wait, show rate drops and both sides lose, so it is in writing.
  • Commission invoiced on the reconciliation report, monthly. If we want a floor, the retainer slider shows what a small one does to the clinic's numbers. Cap the total at a third of proven revenue if the clinic asks. We will rarely hit it.
  • 90-day pilot, then rolling 30 days. Either side walks with 30 days notice. Ninety days is enough to prove or kill it.
  • Exclusivity by city or radius. One clinic per catchment. It is the reason they should sign quickly and it stops us competing with ourselves in the auction.

Running it from Sofia

Two founders sell, set strategy and own the clinic relationships. One hire in Sofia runs the machine: media buying, creative iteration, funnel and CRM setup, lead quality checks and the weekly report. One person carries 6 to 10 clinics depending on how much of the creative and reporting is templated. Second hire at 8 or so clinics, before the first one is drowning.

We run the sales process, not the clinic. The lead gets an SMS or WhatsApp within a minute and a call from our setter inside fifteen minutes in opening hours. The setter qualifies, explains the diagnostic and its price, books the scan and the consult, takes the payment, sends the reminders, and calls again after the plan presentation. For Clinica Duchev that setter is Bulgarian-speaking and sits in Sofia, which is the whole reason the first hire is there. For the next markets it is a setter per language. This is the piece that makes commission pricing safe: booking rate and attribution are ours to control.

Tooling is cheap. A CRM with sub-accounts per clinic, call tracking numbers per country, Meta, an editing tool for creative, a shared drive of ad templates by treatment type.

Risks that kill the commission model

  • Meta's health data rules. Since 2025 Meta restricts what data sources in health categories can send for optimisation. A dental clinic's pixel or CAPI dataset can get flagged, which limits optimising on lower-funnel custom conversions. Check the data source category in Events Manager on day one. Fallback is the leads objective with instant forms and a qualifying question, which still works.
  • GDPR. Anything about a person's teeth is health data, a special category. Keep forms to name, phone, email and a treatment interest dropdown, with an explicit consent checkbox and a privacy notice naming the clinic as controller. Do not pipe lead details into tools that are not under a processing agreement.
  • Attribution disputes. If the clinic does not share numbers, commission is a handshake. The lead sheet, the monthly reconciliation and a written fallback exist for this. If a clinic refuses to share collected revenue, bill a fixed amount per consult that shows up instead, priced off the same model.
  • Slow follow-up. A lead called after two hours is worth a fraction of one called in five minutes. Most bad results in this category are follow-up problems dressed up as ad problems. Since we run the setting ourselves this one is on us, so the setter's speed to lead is the first number on the weekly report.
  • No capacity. Only sign clinics that can add 5 to 15 high-ticket cases a month. A full diary means long waits, no-shows and a churned client. Ask for chair capacity and surgeon days in the first call.
  • Creative fatigue in small markets. A city of 300,000 burns through a creative set in weeks. Budget for new video every month and reuse winners across clinics in the same language.
