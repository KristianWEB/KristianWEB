## Twelve more, hook frames written, body mapped

7. The Conversion Rate (P1, bucket 1). Source: TH1 9th iteration, never launched, the Sultanic canonical.
F1 There's a reason some people build more wealth on $200,000 than others do on $500,000
F2 It has nothing to do with picking better stocks or working harder. It comes down to how much of the income survives long enough to compound.
Body: paycheck income gets taxed before you can invest it, so you build wealth with what's left; maxing the 401(k), HSA and backdoor Roth are good moves that don't change how the rest is taxed; the CPA is right inside the current setup; the bigger plan uses structures the code already rewards, put in place before the year ends. Number: "I've reviewed more than a thousand tax returns" (reconfirm with Preston) or $85,871 with the three-part breakdown. Pool: a brokerage app, an HYSA balance, a badge, an EV charging in a driveway.

8. I Max Everything (P1). Source: ideas list plus the Conversion Rate body.
F1 You max the 401(k), fund the HSA and do the backdoor Roth every year... and the tax bill barely moves
F2 Those are all good moves. They don't change how the rest of your income is taxed.
Body: everything on the employee side of the code, the other side nobody showed you, the CPA is right within what they've been shown, $19,604. Pool: a contribution-limit screen, a standing desk, a nice normal kitchen.

9. The $100K Parking Lot (P1). Source: ideas list, P1 profile ($100K to $500K idle in savings).
F1 There's $100,000 sitting in a savings account because you ran out of ideas
F2 Your CPA says you're doing everything you can. Inside your current setup, that's true.
Body: the money is parked because every employee-side tool is already used; the other side of the code is where it goes next; before year-end. Pool: a bank app balance, a coffee, a parking garage.

10. Thirty Years In, Still Not Sure You Can Stop (P3 and P6). Source: Concept 2, buyer quote.
F1 After 30 years of work, a 59-year-old high earner told us: "I want to be done. But I can't say I'm financially prepared for that."
F2 The question had changed and nobody had answered it: what replaces the paycheck?
Body: the paycheck has done one job for 30 years; if the household needs $10,000 a month, retirement has to produce it from Social Security, the accounts and the investments together; until you know what those produce, you don't know if the paycheck is optional; figure it out before you leave, while you still have income and time. Pool: the exit-planner pool from 1.

11. What Replaces the Paycheck (P6). Source: Concept 2 H4.
F1 Your household runs on $10,000 a month. What produces that after the paycheck stops?
F2 Part comes from Social Security. Part from the 401(k). Part from whatever else you built. Nobody has added it up.
Body as 10. Pool: a man at 58 in an office at 6pm, a truck in a driveway, a grandkid's game.

12. Give Yourself a Raise (owners with an LLC, P4). Source: Concept 13. Per Eli, owners need the least creative effort, so this is one cell, not a family.
F1 You don't need to work more before you can give yourself a raise
F2 If the business already makes good money, there's another place to find it
Body: same profit next year, less of it leaving in taxes, the difference stays with you; the levers owners have (entity taxation, how you pay yourself, retirement accounts through the business, timing, paper losses); the decisions get handled one at a time by people who never talk; the question changes from "how do I make more" to "how do I keep more." Pool: a service van, a shop floor, a quarterly estimated payment being written, an owner's kitchen table.

13. The $70 a Day Trashcan (warm only). Source: the warm winner, 7.84x. Cold 0.74x, so this never goes on broad.

14. One Hour (warm only). Source: W3 One Hour Math, 6.52x warm.

15. Two Households, Same $300K. Source: ideas list. The Sort at the household level, the P2 mirror.
